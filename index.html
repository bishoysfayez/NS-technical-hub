Department of Quality

Sustainable Improvement in Water Management: Nile Sugar Case Study

Authors: Nile Sugar Quality Team
Affiliation: Nile Sugar Company (Quality Department)
Abstract

Nile Sugar’s water management programme addressed rising freshwater demand and the quality of factory effluent during capacity expansion. Beginning in 2010, the plant tested chemical clarification, reused cooling water and surplus condensate, stabilized wastewater loads through buffer storage, and upgraded biological treatment. A decolorization unit, polishing pond, filtration and dedicated condensate cooling were brought into operation by 2016. These measures enabled treated water to be reused in factory operations while reducing the quantity discharged. Raw water consumption fell from 1.27 m³ per tonne of beet in 2010 to 0.045 m³ in 2025. The project also reduced pumping and treatment costs and strengthened operational resilience during interruptions to the external water supply.

Key Highlights & Results
  • Raw water cut from 1.27 to 0.045 m³ / ton beet
  • New dedicated cooling tower for surplus condensate
  • Decolorization & polishing pond implementation
  • ~350 tonnes CO₂e avoided per campaign

Department of Research & Development (R&D)

Our Road to Sustainability: Energy Management in Nile Sugar Factory

Authors: Nile Sugar R&D Team
Affiliation: Nile Sugar Company (R&D Department)
Abstract

This presentation examines Nile Sugar’s approach to reducing energy intensity through a structured energy management programme aligned with ISO 50001. The work combines performance measurement, target setting and continual improvement with practical measures in maintenance and production. These include vibration analysis, in-house fan balancing, thermal inspections, automated energy reporting, tighter process-water control and heat-loss reduction. Reported results include a decline in natural gas use from 44.13 to approximately 37 m³ per tonne of beet and electricity use from 35.52 to 29.5 kWh per tonne of beet. Boiler, pulp-dryer and lime-kiln gas consumption also improved. The presentation reports cumulative savings of approximately EGP 290 million over 2020–2025.

Key Highlights & Results
  • ISO 50001 Certified Energy Management
  • Natural gas reduced to ~37 m³ / ton beet
  • Custom SharePoint Energy Tracking App
  • Cumulative savings of ~EGP 290 Million

Department of Mechanical Maintenance

The Financial and Sustainable Impact of Pump Dynamics in Sugar Industry

Authors: Nile Sugar Mechanical Maintenance Team
Affiliation: Nile Sugar Company (Mechanical Maintenance)
Abstract

This study describes two pump improvement projects at Nile Sugar, linking hydraulic performance, reliability and operating cost. In the first, the engineering team addressed pump limitations during factory capacity expansion through speed reduction, impeller redesign and suction-piping modifications instead of full equipment replacement. The presentation reports 1.12 GWh saved per campaign, equivalent to approximately EGP 6.4 million, with an estimated three-week payback. The second project replaced single mechanical seals with double seals using API Plan 54 barrier systems to prevent water entering syrup streams. Reported benefits include reduced steam losses, improved product integrity and seal service life increasing from six to more than 24 months. Together, the cases demonstrate the value of targeted maintenance engineering.

Key Highlights & Results
  • 1.12 GWh saved per campaign (~EGP 6.4M)
  • Ultra-fast 3-week financial payback
  • API Plan 54 double seal integration
  • Seal service life increased from 6 to 24+ months
